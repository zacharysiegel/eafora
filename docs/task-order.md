## Sequence

1. **Schema version backtracking** — a published manifest at a schema version a deployed client cannot read currently stops that client updating entirely, because the live path fetches only `latest/manifest.json`. Publishing an index of versions and having the client open the newest one it understands removes the deploy-order constraint that a schema bump imposes today. Detail in `docs/backlog.md` §Client. Ordered here at the owner's direction, immediately after the region detail dock. **In progress** (`manifest-schema-backtracking`).
2. **Restyle the year scrubber on the scroll thumb idiom** — the detail dock's scroll thumb settled a vocabulary the rest of the interface does not yet use: a hairline capsule with circular caps, an interior that opens to paper white behind one border when grabbed, a width that animates in and out, and no reserved gutter. The scrubber still wears the earlier vocabulary, a round red thumb on a 1px rail with the active period drawn as a separate 3px overlay. Bringing it across raises three questions the dock did not have to answer, because a scroll thumb may vanish and a year control may not: how much of the control may recede when the pointer is elsewhere, whether the subject is the active year or the period's span (a cohort statistic covers a range, so the span cannot be dropped), and whether the interaction can stay on a real `input[type=range]`, which is what gives it keyboard access today. The design is chosen. The mark becomes a capsule with circular caps whose length is the active period, drawn in ink rather than the accent so the scrubber speaks the dock thumb's vocabulary and red stays with the map's data. At rest the capsule is exactly the period's extent, so the reading never lies; on hover or grab it swells 5px in each direction and its interior opens to paper white behind one hairline, growing symmetrically so its centre does not move. The one-year case floors at 3px so an annual period is still a capsule rather than a line. The capsule's length comes from the data, which no native range thumb can express, so the remaining decision is whether to hand-write the drag and the arrow keys or graft the capsule onto the existing `input[type=range]` and keep them free. Touches `web/style/components/_controls.scss` and the scrubber markup in `web/src/map/controls.rs:88`. Ordered here at the owner's direction, before the iOS client.
3. **`004-ios-client`** — the iOS surface: a UniFFI boundary, a SwiftUI shell, and the Metal surface. Spec, plan, and tasks in `specs/004-ios-client/`. **In progress** (`004-ios-client`), planning only so far.
   - Delivered in phases per `plan.md` §Phasing for PRs. Phases 0.1, 0.2, A, and B carry task breakdowns; C and D are scoped sketches that need one before being picked up.
   - Phase 0.1 is blocked on approving `uniffi`, the feature's one new Rust dependency. Phase D is blocked on an Apple Developer Program enrollment, which gates device installs, TestFlight, and Universal Links but nothing on the simulator.
   - Phase 0.2 moves the web client's load orchestration, discovery reconciliation, and version ranking into `shared`, since all three are platform-agnostic Rust that happens to live under `web/`. Doing it first is what stops the iOS client reimplementing 562 lines of it in Swift.
